# Solstice audit · September 2026

Phase 1 of the deep audit. Read-only: nothing in the app was changed, deployed, or written to any device. Everything below is anchored to a file and line in the repo at commit `7cb3eef` and to the live site as it rendered on 2026-09-25 between 07:25 and 07:35 CDT.

## How this was produced

- Nine audit finders (one per dimension) read the whole server and web codebase; every finding was then re-read and challenged by an independent verifier that had to refute it. 192 candidate findings went in, 183 survived, 9 were refuted. A second "what did the first pass miss" round added the findings marked *round 2* below.
- Six design panels (two independent designers plus a judge each) produced the componentization, learning-layer, enhancements, tests, visualization and share-view designs in Part C.
- I walked every tab of the live site at phone width (375 × 812) and on the desktop stage, in normal, night (07:25, sun 1° up) and outage-preview states, and read the live API responses (GET only).
- Severity: **critical** = device control, money leak, data loss or a security hole; **high** = wrong numbers shown to you or failures likely in normal use; **medium** = degraded behaviour, performance or maintainability with real cost; **low** = polish. Effort: S under an hour, M half a day, L multi-day.

## Executive summary

**The app is good at what it shows and unprotected in what it allows.** Solstice runs in single-owner mode with no owner identity at all, so every `/api` route is open to anyone who has the URL. That is one root cause behind fifteen of the findings: anyone can rewrite the pump schedules on the Pentair controller, set the Nest thermostat, flip both Autopilots to Auto, delete or poison bills, read the system price, loan terms, monthly payment, every PEC bill including the account number, 437 days of five-minute occupancy-revealing usage, gateway and Powerwall serial numbers, and can even complete the Tesla OAuth flow with their own account and knock the owner's Tesla link offline. This is not theoretical: the live `/api/settings` response returned the loan terms during the walkthrough. The fix is one new layer, not accounts: an owner cookie issued once by a magic link, an allow-list of guest-safe fields enforced on the server, and owner-only guards on every write. That same layer is the foundation the share view needs, so it is Batch 1 of Phase 2.

**Both Autopilots are in Auto on the live site.** Pool and AC settings both read `autopilot: "auto"`, and the AC log shows three setpoint writes since yesterday. Your rule says they stay in Suggest unless you say otherwise. Either you set this, or it was set through the open settings endpoint. It is question 1.

**The models are directionally right but numerically shaky.** The learned AC draw (2.0 kW) is about half the real figure because each Nest transition is matched to the five-minute bucket that contains the switch. The AC plan can oscillate the setpoint every five minutes because it re-plans with live humidity and no hysteresis. The web and the server compute two different "heat model" slopes, the pool plan and Autopilot pick the run window from two different solar profiles that are an hour apart, the Day Ring's AC segment is not the AC, and the "kWh saved" headline is built from three unrelated constants. Part C's learning layer turns these into measured, scored quantities with confidence shown in the UI.

**Data has quiet holes.** Every DST-start day is fetched with a window that crosses midnight and comes back empty (2026-03-08 has zero rows and is marked complete), the last hour of every fall-back day is never fetched, a day is marked synced even when Tesla returns nothing, live readings and outage detection only happen while the app is open, and "Tesla not reporting" is judged by the fetch time rather than the reading's own timestamp.

**The phone experience has one bad bug and a layer of regressions.** OrbitControls sets `touch-action:none` on six full-width canvases, so on a real phone a finger that lands on any 3D card rotates the model instead of scrolling the page (a third of every tab is a scroll dead zone). Three CSS selectors were redefined when the Insights panels were added, which silently restyled the Now and History stat tiles, wrapped the status chips, and made the History range control sticky. Several card headers and labels overflow at 375 px. All of these are in the sixteen-fix mockup `mockups/j-ui-fixes.html`.

**Performance is paid for in the wrong places.** One 743 kB JavaScript chunk, seven WebGL contexts created at boot for tabs never opened, a full-screen seven-octave aurora shader behind a 34 px backdrop blur on the phone, two 2048² shadow passes every frame, ~100 Neon queries per minute while the app is open, a schema check of 20 DDL statements on every cold start, and a `learnAcKw` routine that issues two un-indexed queries per thermostat transition on every AC read and every five-minute cron tick. The last one will start hitting Vercel's 60 s limit within about two weeks of Nest history.

Ranked by impact × likelihood, with the fix and effort. Full per-dimension tables with every confirmed finding are in Appendix A.

1. **[critical] Anyone can write to the pool controller and the thermostat.** `POST /api/appliances/pool/apply`, `/apply-tomorrow`, `/restore`, `/pool/autopilot`, `POST /api/appliances/ac/settings`, `/ac/apply` and `PUT /api/settings` sit behind `requireUser`, which in single-owner mode authenticates nothing ([app.ts:335](server/src/app.ts:335), [app.ts:384](server/src/app.ts:384), [auth.ts:48](server/src/auth.ts:48)). A settings PUT can also choose which circuits the next apply deletes and what RPM it writes, and an AC settings POST with `presence` triggers `acTick` immediately, so a 50 °F target reaches `setCool` in the same request. *Fix:* owner guard on every write route; validate and clamp pool RPM to the pump's min/max and AC targets to a fixed 65–85 °F window in `applyPlan` and `acTick` as defence in depth; require the plan hash from the GET in the apply body. Effort M.
2. **[critical] Anyone can replace the owner's Tesla link.** `/auth/login` mints `signState(0)` for any caller and the callback overwrites the single `tesla_accounts` row ([app.ts:80](server/src/app.ts:80), [tesla/auth.ts:20](server/src/tesla/auth.ts:20)). ## Part C · Designs

Each topic was designed twice from different angles and merged by a judge that re-checked every claim against the code. The full designs (with signatures, SQL, thresholds and provenance) are in `docs/audit-designs/` next to this report; this part is the summary you need to decide.

### C1 · Owner identity and the share view (mockup `q-share.html`)

**Access model.** Link-based guest tokens, plus a real owner preview. No accounts, no passwords, no user table.
- Owner: an `OWNER_KEY` secret in Vercel env. A magic link `https://<app>/#owner=<key>` (fragment, so it never reaches request logs, Referer headers or link unfurlers) is opened once per device; the client posts it to `POST /api/auth/owner`, which sets an HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax `solstice_owner` cookie backed by a row in a new `access_tokens` table (random id, SHA-256 of the token, sliding 400-day expiry, per-device sign-out). Inside an installed PWA with no address bar, the locked card has an "I'm the owner" field that takes the key from a password manager. If `OWNER_KEY` is unset in production the server fails closed: everyone is anonymous.
- Guest: `POST /api/shares {label, expiresIn, scope}` creates a 192-bit token shown once as `https://<app>/#s=<token>` (and as a QR). Opening it sets a `solstice_guest` cookie for the share's lifetime; links are reusable so a second device or a cleared cookie just re-opens the link. Revoke is a row update and takes effect on the next request; the list shows label, created, expires, opened N×, last used and the redeeming device.
- Anonymous: gets the static shell, `/api/auth/me` and the Fleet API public key. Nothing else.

**Server-side enforcement.** An `identify` middleware sets `req.role`; a write guard makes every non-GET under `/api` owner-only (crons and the import route stay on their bearer tokens; guests may `POST /api/sync` with a 4 s budget so their "today" stays fresh); a deny-by-default `GUEST_GET` allow-list refuses any route not explicitly guest-safe before the handler runs; per-route view functions redact the allowed ones with three shape-preserving operations (leaf → null with a `redacted[]` marker, string → fixed generic text, array filter). Both OAuth starts and callbacks become owner-only, which also closes the Tesla re-link hole. Three redactions happen at the source: the AC plan is computed with presence forced to `home` so guests never learn "away"; `?fresh=1` is ignored for guests; the error handler returns a generic message.

**What a guest sees.** The whole app, same tabs, same card heights. Live kW, kWh, percentages, dates, the twins, the plans and their reasons, Autopilot state as a badge. Money is never a value: every `$` slot renders a frosted `.veil` decoy that keeps the width ("$•.•• at PEC rates", "$•••/mo", "•• yrs"), and cards that are only about money ("Where your last bill went", "This billing cycle", "Monthly cost", "Your system so far") become `.card.locked`: frosted glass over a deterministic skeleton at the owner card's height with a lock glyph. Tapping any veil shows one toast: "Private to the owner · Dollar amounts, bills and controls stay with the owner." Controls are hidden, not disabled (Apply, Restore, Home/Away, cleaning buttons, bill upload, Alerts switches, Export, raw data), with one `.fine` line where the button was. Identifiers never leave the server (site id, serials, DINs, Nest device path, firmware, ScreenLogic system name, the ZIP, the AC log lines that mention away). The tariff itself is redacted, and the client's hard-coded rate fallbacks are removed, because rate × kWh rebuilds the bill.

**Owner preview.** A switch in Settings › Sharing. ### C2 · Learning layer (mockup `r-learning.html`)

**What it is.** A `predictions` table (immutable rows keyed by model, target, horizon, day and hour, with the inputs that produced them), a `daily_metrics` table of each day's actuals computed once, weekly `model_scores`, and an `anomalies` table with open/ack/snooze/close. A nightly `/api/cron/learn` at 05:45 Central scores yesterday's predictions, refits the models, evaluates the anomaly rules and writes today's predictions before sunrise; a light `learnTick` at the front of the five-minute cron adds the 17:45 forecast snapshot.

**Models it scores** (with the ground truth each uses): pump watts per (RPM, circuit) from readings; pool kWh/day by integrating readings when coverage ≥ 80 % of pump hours, else marked unscored; pool savings against a counterfactual (the previous schedule × the measured curve), because there are no measured pre-plan days; AC kW as the median step from one `LAG` + `LATERAL` query (replacing the N+1), scored out of sample day by day; AC kWh/day two ways (runtime × kW, and bucket residual over the day's OFF baseline) with a sanity warning when they diverge; an AC runtime model on degree-hours; AC plan savings against matched non-plan days; solar yield on past-day tilted irradiance kept separate from the weather forecast's own error; the 48-hour forecast in three horizon bands; overnight baseline; battery usable capacity from full-discharge segments and 30-day round-trip efficiency; the what-if replay as a weekly calibration.

**Confidence in the UI.** Five badges: `measured` (a direct reading), `learned ±X%` (out-of-sample error inside the ceiling), `estimated ±X%`, `learning · n of N`, `unscored`. Confidence = coverage × (0.75 × accuracy + 0.25 × steadiness) × freshness, tiered at 0.70. Bands are empirical p10–p90. They appear on the 48-hour header and sentence, the Today tiles, the AC draw stat, the pool dial centre, a new "Usable per full discharge" Powerwall row, the Panels expected-vs-actual legend and the Day Ring legend. A Model Report card on Insights › Home lists the nine models with an eight-week sparkline, an improvement headline once two models have one, a learning log, and a tap-through sheet with the last 30 predicted-vs-actual pairs.

**Anomaly rules** (each with an explicit threshold, persistence, confound checks and auto-close): pump below its clean-filter baseline (a loaded filter or closed valve draws *less* at fixed RPM; both designers had the physics backwards and the judge corrected it), pump prime loss, pump above baseline, pool schedule drift, AC overrun vs the runtime model (suppressed after a setpoint drop or on away days), strip-heat stacking (with a back-test on last winter's buckets), solar step-down, one-panel loss (~3 %), dust, year-over-year degradation, battery fade and low round-trip, overnight creep (fast and slow), and model drift.

**Re-planning.** Pump-curve refits are automatic (numbers only). AC coast/depth trims are learned from the indoor-temperature trajectory and offered as a "Use this" suggestion (question 21 asks whether they may apply automatically). The solar trim factor and the forecast home-profile shadow model promote automatically.

Items (21), all serverless-cheap: the AC-learner rewrite alone removes about 1,000 Neon queries per open hour. Two need your yes: read-only pool sampling from the cron (~38 reads a day during pump hours) and AC control days (hold the band on 1 in 5 eligible days so savings can be measured).
